How we built the LLM engine at LetsData and process 1B+ tokens per day several times cheaper than API models of similar quality.

A year and a half ago I joined LetsData and was selecting models to power our AI processing at scale, starting with replacing old-school ML translators with LLMs. After a lot of evals, open-source and API, Gemini 2.0 Flash won by a wide margin on quality per dollar - at $0.40 per 1M output tokens it became our workhorse for translation and for everything we built on top: topic modeling, summarization, image and video description, anomaly detection and validation, narrative extraction.

But there is a twist with API models - they live a short life, 12-18 months. And sometimes the replacement costs a few times more, which breaks unit economics at scale. On February 18, 2026, Google deprecated Gemini 2.0 Flash, with shutdown on June 1.

🔀 The options (as of spring 2026):
→ Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite. About three times the price. Quality comparable to 2.0 Flash. The other Flash models cost even more.
→ Other providers. Claude Haiku and GPT-5 mini cost more still. Every prompt we run is calibrated on Gemini, so a switch meant re-evaluating and rewriting all of them.
→ Self-hosted open-source models. The one we picked.

🔍 Model selection story:
I started with Qwen 3.x models. They have decent quality on agentic tasks with thinking enabled, but they are slow and burn a lot of tokens. With thinking disabled, quality drops a lot. And their hybrid architecture (Gated DeltaNet + attention) makes prefix caching less precise: with FP8 KV cache and MTP, vLLM sizes the KV block to 1,600 tokens on Qwen3.8 27B, so shorter prompts get zero cache hits.

But everything changed on April 2, 2026, when Google released Gemma 4. Around the same time Google Cloud made G4 instances with the RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell available - 96 GB of VRAM, including fractional GPUs. That changed both the quality equation and the math.

⚡ The levers to get the maximum out of a self-hosted model while keeping quality almost unchanged:
→ Optimize concurrency and the available KV cache
→ Run the KV cache in FP8
→ Use MTP speculative decoding with tuned parameters
→ Use NVFP4 weights when possible. The catch: a proper NVFP4 build is mixed precision - attention and some MLPs in FP8, the rest NVFP4. That gives a smaller docker image, more KV cache headroom and higher throughput than FP8.

I did the same manually from the Google QAT checkpoint - NVFP4 MLPs, FP8 attention, calibrated on our own traffic. Same quality on most tasks, but still some drop on low-resource language translation. Then the Unsloth build arrived with the more refined recipe, and we moved to it: ~30% more throughput at 128 parallel requests than the FP8 model, with spare GPU memory to co-host embedding models.

📊 All numbers re-measured on vLLM 0.31 with FP8 KV cache and MTP on, using our production translation prompt - per model, at 1 request and 128 parallel, in the image.

💰 Pricing comes down to what load you have, and whether it fits self-hosted models. We process social media data: an almost steady flow of requests with occasional bursts for heavier processing, so queue-based autoscaling on Kubernetes fits well.

Current Spot prices on Google Cloud: $1,189 per month for a full GPU (g4-standard-48), $683 for a half (g4-standard-24). Converted into $/MTok with our production profile - 5:1 input:output split, input priced at 1/5 of output, average load at 50% of peak throughput - the comparable Gemma models come out 32-46% cheaper than Qwen, and Gemma 4 26B-A4B lands at $0.064 per 1M output tokens against $1.50 for Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite. And unlike an API bill, the same G4 node can also host smaller GPU models (an embedding model, in our case), and CPU-heavy jobs can run on its spare 48 vCPUs.
